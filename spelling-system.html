Tujia spelling system

A practical reference to the Draft Phonetic Scheme for the Tujia Language, prepared by Peng Xiumo and Ye Deshu at Jishou University.

Scope. Tujia has Northern varieties (including Longshan, Yongshun, Baojing and Guzhang) and a Southern variety in Luxi. This scheme takes Miaoshi in Longshan as its reference point and Northern Tujia as its base.

Principles

  1. The scheme uses Latin letters and follows Hanyu Pinyin conventions where practical.
  2. Words, rather than individual syllables, are the basic units of writing.
  3. Modern Chinese loans follow the local Southwestern Mandarin pronunciation while remaining close to Hanyu Pinyin spelling.

Tones

A tone letter is written at the end of each syllable. Citation forms mark the basic tone rather than the changed tone.

xTone 1 · 55
high level · rax
rTone 2 · 21
low falling · rar
vTone 3 · 53
high falling · rav
fTone 4 · 35
mid rising · raf

When two high-level syllables occur together, the second may surface with a mid-level value of 33. This is tone sandhi, not a fifth basic tone.

Using the scheme in this project

The dictionary combines data from several publications and transcription systems. Northern records can be shown in the draft spelling. Southern Tujia records retain their source IPA and any converted spelling is labelled as a reference. Records that STEDT does not classify as Northern or Southern are marked “Dialect unspecified”.
